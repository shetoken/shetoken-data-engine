# LinkedIn Post

What if the world's progress on women's rights wasn't just a moral argument — but a number you could look up, like a stock price?

That's what I just built.

It's a data engine at the centre of which sits the Women's Empowerment Index: a single 0–100 score for 105 countries, built from eight pillars — empowerment, education, economic inclusion, health, bodily autonomy, safety, dignity, and digital participation. Violence against women is a penalty that subtracts from the score, because no country should be able to offset femicide with GDP.

Around it, seven more indexes measure what the headlines miss: the unpaid care work no one counts, sexual violence measured by health surveys instead of under-reported police stats, women's exposure to AI job loss, the invisible crisis of widows, and women's voice in media and civil society.

Three things I care about most:

→ It's honest. Some newer data ships as transparent modeled estimates — and every record says so. You can audit every number and challenge any score. The methodology is open.

→ It's alive. A weekly agent scans 139+ news sources in 15 languages, classifies each story for its impact on women, and nudges the relevant scores. A crackdown on women journalists moves the Voice score down this week. A new rights law moves it up.

→ It runs itself. Generators, a public API, automated weekly and monthly updates — fully separated layers, deployed and live.

The principle underneath all of it: what gets measured gets managed. "Things are getting better for women" is easy to ignore. "This score dropped 3 points after that law" is not.

The data is flowing. Next, I'm building the place where the world can see it.
